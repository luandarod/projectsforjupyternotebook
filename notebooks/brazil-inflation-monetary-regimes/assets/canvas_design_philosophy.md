# Monetary Atlas

Monetary Atlas treats macro data as a slow-moving map of pressure. The design should feel calm, measured and institutional, with enough imperfection to show that the economy is not a clean diagram. Every line must look meticulously crafted, as if the final spacing came from many rounds of trimming.

Form comes from corridors, regime bands and pressure fields. Inflation, exchange rate and policy rates should appear as related forces moving through the same canvas. The composition needs master-level execution in alignment, scale and negative space, with no chart clutter pretending to be detail.

Color should be restrained. Use one pressure color, one policy color, one neutral line and one quiet accent. The palette should feel like a central-bank archive filtered through editorial design. It needs painstaking attention to contrast because the piece is about reading small changes without shouting.

Typography stays sparse. Labels should behave like annotations in a macro research note, not like slide copy. Text anchors the viewer, but the visual sequence carries the argument. The final carousel should feel like the product of deep expertise and careful removal.

The emotional center is caution. A regime model can name long phases in IPCA, FX and Selic, but it is not a central-bank model. The viewer should leave with a sense of structure and with the caveat still visible in the room.
