# Regime Weather

Regime Weather treats markets as changing atmospheric systems. The design should not feel like a trading dashboard. It should feel like a carefully observed weather map for uncertainty: thin contours, quiet trajectories, small labels and wide areas of silence. The final work must look meticulously crafted, with each point, band and interval placed after many rounds of refinement.

Form comes from motion and state changes. Curves should drift. Clusters should feel discovered rather than decorated. Four regimes can appear as distinct climates, but the borders must stay porous because market data rarely gives clean walls. The craftsmanship lives in that restraint: master-level execution without visual shouting.

Color should carry regime identity without turning the piece into a neon finance graphic. Use a narrow palette with one stress accent, one calm accent, one momentum accent and one neutral anchor. The colors should feel measured and intentional, the product of painstaking calibration rather than excitement.

Typography is sparse and clinical. Labels are small, almost like annotations on a scientific plate. The carousel should tell the story through sequence, space and repeated visual language, not through paragraphs on the image. Every slide should look like part of the same artifact.

The emotional center is caution. A regime model can help name the market state, but it cannot turn uncertainty into prophecy. The viewer should feel the usefulness of the map and the instability of the territory at the same time.
